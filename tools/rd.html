RD calculator: recurring deposit maturity amount and interest

A recurring deposit (RD) lets you put away a fixed amount every month, in a bank or at the post office, and collect the total plus interest when the term ends. This RD calculator takes three inputs, your monthly deposit in rupees, the annual interest rate and the number of months, and shows the maturity value, the total you deposited and the interest you earn.

It is useful before you open an RD, when you want to compare a 2 year plan with a 5 year plan, or when you simply want to know what a fixed monthly saving turns into.

How to use this RD calculator

  1. Type your monthly deposit, for example 5000.
  2. Enter the interest rate your bank or post office offers, in percent per year.
  3. Enter the tenure in months: 12 for one year, 24 for two years, 60 for five years.
  4. Read the maturity value, total deposited and interest earned. Results update as you type, so you can try a few combinations quickly.

The RD maturity formula used here

M = P × [(1 + i)n − 1] ÷ i × (1 + i)

P is the monthly deposit, i is the annual rate divided by 12 and by 100, and n is the number of months. This calculator compounds monthly and treats every deposit as made at the start of its month.

Most Indian banks compound RD interest quarterly and have their own rules for deposit dates, so the maturity figure printed on your RD receipt can differ slightly from this estimate. Treat the result as a planning number and confirm the final figure with your bank.

RD calculation examples

These examples use sample rates only, not current bank rates:

  • ₹5,000 a month for 24 months at 7%: you deposit ₹1,20,000 and receive about ₹1,29,154, so the interest is about ₹9,154.
  • ₹1,000 a month for 12 months at 6.5%: you deposit ₹12,000 and receive about ₹12,431, so the interest is about ₹431.
  • ₹10,000 a month for 60 months at 7%: you deposit ₹6,00,000 and receive about ₹7,20,105, so the interest is about ₹1,20,105.

RD vs FD: which one fits you

An FD needs the full amount on day one. An RD is built for people who do not have a lump sum but can set aside money every month, such as a salaried person saving for a fee, a trip or an emergency fund. Because each RD instalment earns interest only for the months it stays in the account, the interest you earn on your total deposit is lower than an FD at the same rate over the same period. If you already have a lump sum, try the FD calculator. If you want market-linked growth instead of a fixed rate, compare with the SIP calculator.

Post office RD

India Post runs its own recurring deposit scheme, usually for a 5 year (60 month) term, with a rate that the government reviews every quarter. Enter the current rate from the India Post website and 60 months to estimate your maturity amount here, then check the official figure for your account.

Frequently asked questions

How is the RD maturity amount calculated?

Using M = P × [(1 + i)^n − 1] ÷ i × (1 + i), where P is the monthly deposit, i is the monthly rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100) and n is the number of months. This calculator uses monthly compounding.

Will this match my bank's RD maturity value exactly?

Not always. Many banks compound RD interest quarterly and follow their own deposit-date rules, so their figure can differ slightly from this estimate. Use it for planning and confirm the final number with your bank.

Is RD interest taxable in India?

Yes, interest on a recurring deposit is taxable as income. Banks may deduct TDS once your interest crosses the limit set under the Income Tax Act. Limits change, so check the current rules or ask a chartered accountant.

What is the usual RD tenure?

It depends on the bank. Many banks offer terms from a few months up to around 10 years, and the post office RD typically runs for 5 years. Enter any number of months in the calculator to see the result.

Does this RD calculator store my numbers?

No. The calculation runs in your browser and nothing is uploaded to a server.
