Old vs New regime: rules used (FY 2026-27)

New regime: nil up to 4 lakh, 5% to 8 lakh, 10% to 12 lakh, 15% to 16 lakh, 20% to 20 lakh, 25% to 24 lakh and 30% above. Standard deduction 75,000. Rebate makes tax nil up to 12 lakh taxable income, with marginal relief just above it.

Old regime: basic exemption 2.5 lakh (3 lakh for 60+, 5 lakh for 80+), 5% / 20% / 30% slabs at 5 lakh and 10 lakh, standard deduction 50,000, rebate of up to 12,500 when taxable income is 5 lakh or less. Deductions you enter (80C capped at 1.5 lakh, 24(b) at 2 lakh, 80CCD(1B) at 50,000, plus HRA, 80D and others) reduce taxable income.

FAQ

Which financial year does this calculator use?

FY 2026-27 (assessment year 2027-28 under the old Act terminology). Budget 2026 left the new regime slabs, the 12 lakh rebate limit and the 75,000 standard deduction unchanged from FY 2025-26.

What is the zero-tax limit in the new regime?

Taxable income up to 12 lakh is fully rebated for a resident individual. For a salaried person with the 75,000 standard deduction, that means gross salary up to 12.75 lakh pays no tax. Just above that, marginal relief caps the tax at the amount earned over 12 lakh.

Does it include surcharge and cess?

Yes. 4% health and education cess is added. Surcharge applies above 50 lakh (10%), 1 crore (15%) and 2 crore (25%); the old regime goes up to 37% above 5 crore. Marginal relief on surcharge is applied.

What does it assume?

Salaried resident individual, standard deduction of 75,000 (new) and 50,000 (old), and only income taxed at normal slab rates. Capital gains at special rates, employer NPS under 80CCD(2) and other special cases are not modelled.

Is this tax advice?

No. It is an estimate for comparing the two regimes. Check the final figure against the Income Tax Department's e-filing calculator or a chartered accountant before filing.
