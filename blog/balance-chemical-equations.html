How to balance a chemical equation: the method, three worked examples

Updated 2026-10-20 · FreeToolHub

A balanced equation has the same number of each kind of atom on both sides. You do it by changing the coefficients (the big numbers in front of a formula). You never change the small numbers inside a formula, because that would turn it into a different substance.

The method

  1. Write the unbalanced equation with the correct formulas.
  2. Count each element on both sides.
  3. Start with the element that appears in the fewest compounds. Leave hydrogen and oxygen for last, since they often appear in several.
  4. Adjust coefficients one at a time and recount after every change.
  5. Reduce to the smallest whole numbers, then do a final count of every element.

Example 1: propane burning

C3H8 + O2 → CO2 + H2O

Carbon first: 3 C on the left, so put 3 in front of CO2. Hydrogen next: 8 H on the left, so put 4 in front of H2O. Now count oxygen on the right: 3 × 2 + 4 = 10, so put 5 in front of O2.

C3H8 + 5 O2 → 3 CO2 + 4 H2O

Example 2: rusting iron

Fe + O2 → Fe2O3

Oxygen is 2 on the left and 3 on the right, so use the common multiple 6: that is 3 O2 and 2 Fe2O3. Now iron on the right is 4, so put 4 in front of Fe.

4 Fe + 3 O2 → 2 Fe2O3

Example 3: aluminium in acid

Al + HCl → AlCl3 + H2

Chlorine: AlCl3 has 3, so HCl needs a 3 for now. Then hydrogen on the right is 2, which does not divide 3, so double everything: 2 Al, 6 HCl, 2 AlCl3, 3 H2.

2 Al + 6 HCl → 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2

Mistakes that cost marks

  • Changing a subscript, such as writing H2O2 to fix oxygen. That is hydrogen peroxide, not water.
  • Forgetting that a coefficient multiplies the whole formula, including everything inside brackets.
  • Stopping before the final recount. Count every element once more at the end.
  • Leaving a common factor, such as 2, 4 and 6, when 1, 2 and 3 would work.

Count test. For 2 Al + 6 HCl → 2 AlCl3 + 3 H2: Al is 2 and 2, H is 6 and 6, Cl is 6 and 6. If every row matches, it is balanced.

Check with the tool

Paste your equation into the Chemical Equation Balancer with an arrow (->) or = between the sides. It solves the coefficients with linear algebra, and it is useful for checking your own working. It recognises common elements such as H, C, N, O, Na, K, Ca, Mg, Al, Fe, Cu, Zn, Cl and S, but not every element in the periodic table, so a rare element may need balancing by hand.
